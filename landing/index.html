Why the number matters

Hire desks ask for the off-hire number. Keep it, with the date, time and who you spoke to.

That is the whole job of this tool: the date you worked to, and a record of the call you made.

Where that comes from

The CPA Supplementary Conditions Applicable to Tool and Equipment Hire put it plainly at clause 9.2.3 of the 2021 edition, and in the same words at clause 8.3.3 of the earlier edition: the owner does not charge hire for a period covered by an off-hire number, but the Hirer shall still be responsible for such hire charges for those items if the Hirer is unable to provide the off-hire number. The same conditions say the off-hire goes in writing and each item is identified clearly enough for a unique number to be issued. Read the 2021 conditions.

That wording is the tool and equipment conditions. Plant proper — excavators, dumpers, telehandlers — is usually hired under the CPA Model Conditions, and we have not read the equivalent clause there, so we make no claim about what those conditions say. Your own hire agreement is the one that counts; read it, or ask your hire company.

The anchor is not other apps, it is one missed charging period. In VP plc v Megarry [2012] NIQB 22 the hirers believed a machine was off hire because of an entry reading “off hire” in the hire company’s own phone log. The hire company said that entry meant something else. The hirers had no record of their own, and they lost the point. What the judgment awarded was a forfeited purchase discount rather than hire charges, so we make no claim here about what a forgotten off-hire costs — only that your own record is the one you can rely on.
