# INTERVIEWS.md — experiment E3 field script

**Experiment:** E3 — 15 structured interviews with OUPV / Master under-100 GT holders.
**Tests:** P10, the inferred core problem — *do they know their number, and do they fear the window.*
**Source of thresholds:** `research/deep-dive-mariner.md` §7. **Offer wording:** `validation/OFFER.md`.
**Cost:** $0–$200 (fuel, coffee). **Days:** 10. **Target:** 15 completed interviews.

> **Why this experiment is load-bearing.** Across two research phases we obtained **zero verbatim
> customer quotes about paying for this**. P10 ("I don't know what my number is right now") is marked
> **(inferred)** in the deep dive with "No quote found. This is the inference the whole product rests
> on." E3 is where that stops being an inference. Getting **three attributable quotes** is an explicit
> deliverable of these interviews, equal in weight to the tally.

## 0. The thresholds, copied from §7 — binding before the result is known

| | |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | **≥8/15 cannot state their trailing-5-year day total; ≥5/15 say unprompted they would pay for help** |
| **FAIL** | **≤4/15 cannot state it, or ≥10/15 say "my school/consultant handles it"** |
| **Decision** | **PASS → proceed to E4. FAIL → the pain is a renewal-week pain, not a daily one; park the niche.** |

Three things follow from that wording and they shape the whole script:

1. **PASS needs both halves.** 8 blanks and only 3 unprompted "I'd pay" is not a pass.
2. **There is an indeterminate band.** 5, 6 or 7 blanks is neither PASS nor FAIL. If you land there,
   you report it as indeterminate and the operating partner decides. **Do not round up into a pass.**
3. **"Unprompted" is a hard constraint, not a style note.** You may not ask "would you pay for this?"
   It only counts if they volunteer it before you have described the product. Which means the script
   below never asks it. What the script *does* ask is what they have already paid, to whom, and when —
   past money, which is better evidence than a promise anyway.

## 1. Mom Test discipline — the five rules, and the failure each one prevents

| Rule | What it stops |
|---|---|
| **Talk about their life, not your idea.** You are not allowed to say the words "app", "we're building" or "360 Ledger" until section 7. | Them being polite to you. The whole dataset is worthless once they know what answer would please you. |
| **Ask about the past, not the future.** "What happened last renewal" beats "what would you do next renewal" every time. | Hypotheticals. Nobody can predict their own behaviour, and captains are no exception. |
| **Ask for specifics, not generalities.** Not "is paperwork annoying?" — "walk me through the last letter you wrote." | Agreement. "Yeah, it's a pain" is a conversational reflex, not data. |
| **Never pitch during discovery.** Compliments, enthusiasm and "that's a great idea" get written in a separate box and **scored zero**. | You, mistaking being liked for being paid. |
| **Shut up and let it be awkward.** After the number question (Q6) you say nothing for a full five seconds. | You rescuing them from the silence that *is* the finding. |

**The compliment rule, stated flatly:** a compliment is not data. If an interview produces nothing but
compliments, record it as a completed interview with zero signal and move on. Do not count it toward
anything. In the capture sheet compliments go in a box labelled `FLUFF` and that box is never tallied.

**Things you are not allowed to say during an interview** (each one contaminates the sample):
- "We're building an app that…"
- "Would you use something that…"
- "How much would you pay for…"
- "Most captains tell me…" (you have not interviewed most captains)
- Anything that implies you know whether their sea service is acceptable. Ever. See §10.

## 2. Who counts as a qualified interview

Record as **qualified** only if all three are true:

1. Holds a current or lapsed **OUPV (six-pack)** or **Master under 100 GT** MMC. Credentials above
   100 GT, foreign/MCA tickets and un-credentialed crew are recorded as `OUT-OF-ICP` and tallied
   separately — they are interesting, they are not E3.
2. **Renews their own credential** — i.e. nobody in an office does their paperwork. If an employer's
   HR department files it, they are `OUT-OF-ICP` (and that fact is itself worth recording).
3. They worked at least some paid days on the water in the last 24 months. A credential in a drawer
   has no window problem.

Target mix across the 15, per §7's channel list: gCaptain and TrawlerForum threads, **dock walks at
two marinas**, **one licence school**. Do not take all 15 from one marina — one dock's answer is one
dock's answer. Aim for roughly 5 forum / 7 dock / 3 school and record the actual split.

**Before you post anywhere:** the deep dive §11 is explicit that every forum's posting rules are
**unverified** because the proxy blocked every forum domain in all three research runs. Read
gCaptain's and TrawlerForum's actual rules pages yourself before your first DM or post. A ban on day
one costs more than this experiment is worth.

## 3. Cold opens — you have no insider edge, so do not pretend to one

Every one of these is a cold open with a working captain who owes you nothing. The one thing you have
that works is that you are honest, specific, short, and you are not selling anything yet.

### 3a. The honest one-sentence introduction (use this everywhere, verbatim)

> "I'm building a small iPhone tool for captains who keep their own sea-time records, and before I
> build it I'm trying to talk to fifteen people who actually do it — I'm not selling anything today,
> I just want to know how you handle it."

Why those words: it says what you are (building something, not a captain), what you want (to be told
how it works), and what you will not do (sell). If they ask whether you are a mariner, the answer is
**"No — that's exactly why I'm asking."** Never imply sea time you do not have. Captains spot it in
one question and the interview is over.

### 3b. Dock walk — marina, 15 minutes, before or after a trip

**Where and when.** Fuel dock, charter slips and the dock house, mid-morning on a weekday, or the
hour after the last charter comes in. Not during boarding. Not while they are docking. Look for
six-pack charter boats, delivery captains, launch and small-tow operators.

**The open — spoken, standing on the dock, hands visible, no clipboard:**

> "Morning — sorry to bother you, have you got five minutes? [one-sentence intro from 3a] Are you
> running a six-pack or a hundred-ton?"

**If they say yes:** "Can I buy you a coffee while we talk?" Pay for the coffee. It costs $4 and it
turns an interruption into a conversation. This is the whole $200 budget.

**If they are busy:** "Totally fair. Is there a better hour, or is there someone on this dock who does
all their own paperwork?" — a referral is a commitment (§6), and it is the best outcome of a bad
moment.

**If they say no:** "Appreciate it, have a good one." Walk away. Do not sell the interview.

**If they ask what the tool does — and they will, around minute three:**

> "I'd rather not lead you. Can I ask you four questions first, and then I'll tell you exactly what
> I'm doing and you can tell me if it's useless?"

That sentence protects the sample and almost always works. If they insist, give them one flat line —
*"it counts your sea-service days in a rolling five-year window and prints the summary"* — then
**mark the interview `PITCH-LEAKED` on the capture sheet** and discount its unprompted-pay answer.

**Dock-walk logistics:** marina offices sometimes want you to check in. Do it. Ask the dockmaster for
permission and tell them the truth; dockmasters know which captains do their own books and will point
you at them. That introduction is worth ten cold approaches.

### 3c. Forum DM — gCaptain / TrawlerForum / ScubaBoard

Only DM someone who has **already posted publicly** about sea service, renewal, a letter of service,
or a lost record. Quote their own post back to them. Never bulk-send.

**Subject:** `Question about your renewal post`

**Body — copy this shape, swap in their actual words:**

> Hi [handle] —
>
> You posted in [thread title] about [the specific thing: writing your sea-time letter / your time
> getting kicked back / not being able to find your old discharges]. I'm building a small iPhone tool
> for captains who keep their own sea-time records, and before I build it I'm trying to talk to
> fifteen people who actually do it. I'm not selling anything and there's nothing to buy.
>
> Would you take 15 minutes on the phone, or just answer here if that's easier? I'd mostly like to
> hear what happened the last time you renewed.
>
> Either way — your post was the clearest description of the problem I've read.
>
> [Name], [city]

**Rules for the DM channel:**
- **No link.** Not to a landing page, not to a calculator, not to anything. A link makes it
  promotion, and §11 says forum self-promotion rules are unverified and likely strict.
- **One follow-up, after five days, two sentences max.** Then stop.
- If they answer in the thread in public rather than by DM, that is better, not worse — and it is a
  public, attributable, linkable quote. Ask permission per §7 anyway.
- Expect a 10–20% reply rate off ~40 DMs for 5 interviews. Budget the volume.

### 3d. Licence school — the classroom

Email the school's owner (small businesses; one email reaches the decision-maker — deep dive §11):

> Subject: 15 minutes with your OUPV students
>
> I'm building an iPhone tool that counts sea-service days in the five-year window and prints the
> per-period summary. Before I write any of it I want to be told how your students actually track
> their days today. Could I sit at the back of one class and ask four questions in the break? I'll
> bring coffee for the room and I won't sell anything to anyone.

**Caveat, and it matters:** the school is also the paid incumbent and a potential competitor. Expect
a cool reception, do not show them the offer, and treat anything they say about their students'
willingness to pay as a competitor's testimony, not a customer's. Students in a renewal class are
also a biased sample — they are in renewal week by definition. Cap the school at 3 of the 15 and
flag those three on the tally.

## 4. The script — asked in this order, every time

Read this aloud in order. Deviate to follow a specific story, then come back. The order is designed
so that the number question (Q6) arrives after they are talking freely and before they have any idea
what you are building.

**Q1 — Warm-up / qualification.**
> "What are you running, and what do you do with it — charters, deliveries, towing?"

Also establishes the ICP screen in §2 without an interrogation. Record vessel type, GT if volunteered,
and whether they own it.

**Q2 — The last renewal, in detail. The single best question in this script.**
> "Tell me about the last time you renewed your credential. Start from when you first thought about
> it."

Then shut up. Let them run. Prompts if they stall: *"What did you have to dig up?"* · *"How long did
the whole thing take, start to finish?"* · *"Who did you have to ask for anything?"*

**Q3 — What went wrong.**
> "Did anything go wrong, or come back at you?"

If yes, the follow-ups are the richest material in the experiment:
- "What exactly came back? Do you still have the letter or the email?"
- "What did you have to do to fix it?"
- "How long was it before you were sorted?"
- "Did it cost you any work? Any days you couldn't run?"

> **This is the question that produces the quotes.** The best verbatim we have from the whole research
> phase is a complaint of exactly this shape — *"if you don't list each specifically by date, then they
> don't want/accept sea time letters of over three years"* (gCaptain, `[snippet-only]`,
> https://forum.gcaptain.com/t/any-advise-on-how-to-write-my-license-renewal-sea-service-letter/10487).
> We need three of those in the customer's own voice, attributable. If Q3 produces a story, go to §7
> and ask for the quote **while the story is still in the air**, not at the end of the interview.

**Q4 — What they use today.**
> "Walk me through how a worked day gets recorded right now. What do you actually touch?"

Follow-ups: *"Show me?"* (ask to see the notebook, the spreadsheet, the phone — ask permission to
photograph it, it is the best artefact you will get all day) · *"What happens when you forget a
day?"* · *"Have you ever tried an app for this? What happened?"*

Record verbatim what the current tool is. Code it: `PAPER-LOG` / `SHOEBOX-DISCHARGES` / `SPREADSHEET`
/ `PHONE-NOTES` / `CALENDAR` / `APP(name)` / `NOTHING` / `SOMEONE-ELSE`.

**Q5 — Who else touches your sea time.**
> "Besides you, who else has a hand in your sea time — anyone who writes you a letter, signs
> anything, or keeps a copy?"

Follow-ups: *"Who signs your letter of service?"* · *"If the NMC asked you to prove a day from 2022
tomorrow, who would you have to call?"* · *"Does your school or a consultant touch any of this?"*

This question does three jobs: it detects the §7 FAIL condition (`≥10/15 say "my school/consultant
handles it"`), it tests P8 (the owner-operator **is** the employer, so there is nobody to ask), and
it finds the referral — anyone they name is a person you can ask them to introduce you to (§6).

**Q6 — THE CORE TEST. Ask it exactly like this.**
> "Right now, off the top of your head and without looking anything up — how many days of sea service
> do you have in the last five years?"

**Protocol, and follow it to the letter:**
1. Ask it once. Do not soften it, do not pre-apologise, do not add "roughly".
2. **Say nothing for five seconds.** Count them.
3. If they reach for a phone, a logbook or a laptop: *"That's the answer, actually — don't look it up
   for me. Where would you have gone to find it?"* Record where. **Going to look it up is recorded as
   CANNOT-STATE.**
4. Write down **the exact words of their answer, verbatim**, before you say anything else.
5. Then, and only then, the two follow-ups:
   - *"How close do you think that is?"*
   - *"When was the last time you knew that number?"*

**Classification — one code per interview, and the whole experiment turns on this column:**

| Code | Meaning | Counts as CANNOT-STATE? |
|---|---|---|
| `STATED-FIRM` | A specific number, unhesitating, no lookup. e.g. "About 340, I ran 68 days last season and I've got four seasons behind it." | **No** |
| `STATED-RANGE-TIGHT` | A range they can justify, inside ±10%. | **No** |
| `STATED-RANGE-WIDE` | A range wider than ±10%, or a guess they immediately disown. | **Yes** |
| `CANNOT-STATE` | "No idea." "I'd have to work it out." "Nobody knows that." | **Yes** |
| `WENT-TO-LOOK` | Reached for a record to answer. | **Yes** |
| `DEFLECTED` | "My school has it." "It's in the letter." | **Yes** — and also tick the school/consultant column |
| `N/A` | Out of ICP, or interview aborted. | Not counted either way |

> **Example of a `CANNOT-STATE` answer — this is an illustration written by us for training, not a
> customer quote:** *"Honestly? No clue. I know I'm fine because I work all summer, but if you asked
> me the number I'd have to sit down with the calendar for an hour."*
> **It is written here only to show the grader what the code looks like. Never quote it as a customer.**

**Q7 — What did you pay.**
> "Last time round, did you pay anybody to help with any of this?"

Follow-ups: *"Who, and what did it cost?"* · *"What exactly did they do for the money?"* · *"Would you
use them again?"* · *"Did you pay for the course, or for someone to handle the paperwork?"*

Past money is the strongest evidence in the whole interview and it is Mom-Test-clean because it
already happened. Record **dollar amount, vendor, and what was delivered**. Two licensing
consultancies run as going businesses (deep dive §3, §10) — Q7 tells us whether *our* ICP is who
pays them, or whether that is somebody else's market.

**Q8 — The hole in the window (past tense only).**
> "Have you ever had a stretch where you weren't working much — a winter, an injury, a season off?
> What did that do to your paperwork?"

Tests whether the five-year hole is a felt experience or our theory. Do not explain the window.

**Q9 — The lapse.**
> "Have you ever let anything expire — the MMC, the medical, the TWIC? Or known somebody who did?"

Follow-up if yes: *"What did getting it back involve?"* This is where you find out whether the
$12,100 reactivation figure means anything on a real dock, or whether it is a number that only exists
in trade press. **Record their figure, not ours.** Never quote our anchor at them during discovery.

**Q10 — The magic wand, asked about their day and not our product.**
> "If you could hand one piece of this paperwork to somebody else tomorrow and never see it again,
> which piece?"

**Q11 — Open floor.**
> "What should I have asked you that I didn't?"

**Q12 — The referral (always ask, every interview, no exceptions).**
> "Who else on this dock does all their own paperwork? Would you introduce me?"

## 5. The hand-off: where discovery ends and the ask begins

The interview is **over** after Q12. Draw a line in your notes. Everything after this line is sales,
is recorded separately, and does not touch the tally.

Only now may you describe what you are building, and only if they asked or you intend to make a
commitment ask. When you do, describe it in one breath, flatly, with the honest cost attached:

> "It's an iPhone app called `360 Ledger`. You tap once for a worked day; it keeps a running count of
> your days inside the five-year window you pick, shows the date your window next drops a day, and
> prints a dated summary listing every period individually with the vessel fields the regulation asks
> for. No account and no server — it's on your phone. The part I can't remove is that you have to log
> the day. If you don't log, it knows nothing."

Say the last two sentences. They cost installs and buy honest retention (deep dive §2).

**Then stop talking.** Their unscripted reaction to that paragraph is the second most valuable thing
in the interview. Write it verbatim.

**You may not say,** in this section or any other: that the NMC will accept anything, that they are
eligible, current or qualified, or that a web payment unlocks the app. (`OFFER.md` §4, "Never
promise".) If money comes up, the only terms that exist are the ones in `OFFER.md` — see §6 below and
`validation/DEPOSIT-OPS.md`.

## 6. What counts as a commitment

A commitment is **money, time, or reputation** — something that costs them to give. Nothing else
counts, and the distinction is the difference between E3 producing a pipeline and E3 producing a nice
afternoon.

| Tier | The commitment | How you ask for it |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Money** | They pay **$49** for the hand-built five-year reconstruction, on the spot, by card. | "I do these by hand right now — you send me whatever records you've got in any form, and inside 72 hours I send you back a dated summary. It's $49. Want me to text you the link?" Then follow `validation/CONCIERGE-SOP.md`. |
| **1 — Money** | They put down the **$49**, fully refundable, founding deposit. | Exact terms and the exact sentence you must say at the moment of payment are in `validation/DEPOSIT-OPS.md`. Do not improvise refund or unlock wording. |
| **2 — Time** | A **scheduled call** with a date and a time in both calendars. | "Can I call you Thursday at seven and walk you through what I'd send you?" |
| **2 — Time** | They agree to the **30-day daily Y/N text probe** (E6) with their phone number written down. | See §9 of `CONCIERGE-SOP.md` for the exact ask. |
| **3 — Reputation** | An **introduction** to a named captain, made by them, by text, in front of you. | "Would you text him now and say I'm not selling anything?" |

**Not commitments. Record under `FLUFF` and score zero:**
- "That's a great idea."
- "I'd definitely use that."
- "Send me the link when it's done." *(This is a polite no. Code it `SOFT-NO`.)*
- "My buddy would love this."
- An email address given with no scheduled next step.
- Any amount of enthusiasm.

**The one-line test:** *what did this conversation cost them?* If the answer is nothing, it was not a
commitment.

## 7. Asking for an attributable quote

**Deliverable: three attributable quotes from these 15 interviews.** We have none. The rules in
`CLAUDE.md` §1 are absolute: never invent a statistic, a review count, a quote or a member count;
verbatim quotes must be real and linked or sourced. So they have to come from here.

**Ask the moment the good sentence lands** — usually inside Q3 or Q7 — not at the end:

> "Stop me there — can I write that down exactly how you said it? […read it back verbatim…] Did I get
> it right? Can I use that sentence when I describe this problem to other captains, with your first
> name and 'charter captain, [port]'? Or initials only, or completely anonymous — whichever you'd
> rather. And I'll send it to you before I use it anywhere."

**Record, for every quote, all five fields — a quote missing any of them is unusable:**

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| `QUOTE` | Verbatim. Their words, their grammar, their profanity. You may only cut with `…` and you may never add a word. |
| `ATTRIBUTION` | One of: `Full name + port` / `First name + role + port` / `Initials + role` / `Anonymous — role only`. Whatever they chose. |
| `PERMISSION` | `written` (text/email saying yes — get this) or `verbal, witnessed by nobody` (weaker; mark it). Written beats verbal; a text message back saying "yep that's fine" is written. |
| `SCOPE` | What they agreed to: internal notes only / landing page / in-app / press. Default to the narrowest they said and never widen it. |
| `DATE + CHANNEL` | Date, and dock-walk / forum DM (with thread URL) / call / school. |

**Rules, no exceptions:**
- If they decline attribution, the quote still goes in the file marked `ANONYMOUS — internal only`.
  It informs our writing. It never appears in public copy as a customer quote.
- **Never paraphrase a customer and present it as a quote.** If you only have the gist, write
  `paraphrase, not a quote` beside it, in those words.
- **Never write a sentence a customer did not say and attribute it to one.** Every hypothetical
  customer sentence in this file is labelled as our own example. Keep that habit in every document
  that comes out of E3.
- A public forum post you are quoting still gets a permission ask by DM, plus the thread URL.

- Total money collected, and from whom.
- Actual hours and dollars spent against the $0–$200 / 10-day budget.

## 10. Standing rules for the field

1. **You never state a conclusion about anyone's standing.** Not on a dock, not on a call, not as a
   favour. If a captain asks "do you think I've got enough days?", the answer is: *"I'm not going to
   tell you that — I'd be guessing about your licence. What I can do is lay out your own days against
   the published interval and show you the citation, and you decide."* (`CLAUDE.md` §4; `OFFER.md` §8.)
2. **Every price, refund term and guarantee clause comes from `validation/OFFER.md`, verbatim.** If
