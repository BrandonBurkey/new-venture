# First interview — one subcontractor owner, five questions

**Due 2026-10-31.** This is the cheapest decisive step in the venture. Everything about candidate
B1 (plant hire off-hire) now turns on facts only a real owner can supply, and no amount of searching
will produce them: a scout spent six targeted attempts looking for a verbatim owner complaint and
found none.

## Who to ask for

A **subcontractor owner** — the person who IS the business, sole trader up to roughly 20 staff, who
hires plant. Not a site manager, not a QS, not an employed plant controller. The site manager is the
introduction, not the interview.

Ask your contact for: *"someone who runs their own gang or small firm and hires their own plant."*

## Mom Test rules — read before you dial

1. **Ask about their past, never about your idea.** "What happened last time" beats "would you use".
2. **Do not pitch.** If they find out you are building something, every answer afterwards is
   politeness. No app is mentioned.
3. **Ask for numbers and names**, not opinions. "Which hirers" not "do you use many hirers".
4. **Dig into the bad news.** If they say it is not a problem, that is the most valuable answer in
   the call. Write it down and thank them.
5. **Shut up and let them talk.** Silence gets the real answer.

## The five questions, in this order

1. **Right now, how many different hire companies have your kit? Name them.**
2. **Where is the list?**
   - "In my head", or "on a delivery note in the van" → the gap is real.
   - "All through one account" → B1 is dead, and that is a result, not a failure.
3. **When you off-hire, who do you ring — and do you write the off-hire number down anywhere?**
4. **Has a hire invoice ever run past the date you thought you had stopped it? What did it cost you,
   and did you get it credited?**
5. **Have you heard of YardLink? Would you order through a platform to get one list, or do you need
   your own rates with your own local depot?**

Question 5 is the one that decides it. A free competitor already consolidates hire across suppliers,
but **only for kit ordered through that competitor**. If owners keep direct credit accounts with
local depots for their own rates, the gap survives. If they are happy to order through a platform,
it does not.

## Pre-committed kill trigger — written before any answers arrive

Per Lean innovation accounting, these thresholds are fixed now so they cannot be moved later to
rescue a favourite:

**B1 is dead, permanently, if either of these is true across ten owner conversations:**
- fewer than **six of ten** name **two or more** concurrent hire companies; or
- **three or more** say the off-hire number is already recorded somewhere they can find it.

**B1 proceeds to a concierge test if:**
- six or more name two or more concurrent hirers, **and**
- at least four describe a specific invoice that ran past their intended off-hire date, **and**
- fewer than three would order all hire through a single platform.

One conversation does not settle this. It tells us whether the next nine are worth having.

## What is already known, so you do not re-ask it

| Question | Answer, with its source |
| --- | --- |
| Does an owner personally carry this cost? | **Yes.** *VP plc v Megarry* [2012] NIQB 22: a partnership pursued for £26,155.50 on one machine, defence "we thought it was off hire", judgment £13,331 plus interest and costs. `[verified-fetch]` |
| Do hire desks benefit from the silence? | An agency selling to hire desks: depots "are in no hurry to fix this". `[verified-fetch]` |
| Is the off-hire number the pivot? | **Yes.** CPA Supplementary Conditions 2011 cl 7.2.3: the hirer pays anyway if he cannot produce the off-hire number. `[verified-fetch]` |
| Is Re-flow the competitor? | **No.** £39 per user per month, one plan, demo-only entry, mid-market civils. Not a sole trader's phone. `[verified-fetch]` |
| Is there a rules engine to build? | **No.** Hire charging terms are commercial, not statutory. This is user-entered arithmetic, so there is no cited rule set and no 60-second browser aha. A real weakness in the product shape. |
