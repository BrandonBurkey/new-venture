# Scoring rubric and hard gates

**Fill in the weights and commit this file BEFORE any research returns.** That is the whole point:
it is the difference between scoring and rationalising. The last venture locked its weights first
and it stopped a favourite idea from being nudged up the table.

| Criterion | Weight | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pain £ (cost of one mistake) | 20% | < £500 | £2k–£10k | > £25k, or recurring loss |
| Audience wealth / purchasing power | 10% | employee on PAYE | owner of a small firm | owner with real capital at risk |
| Competitive weakness (blue ocean) | 15% | incumbent nails it | 2–4 mediocre tools | nothing credible; spreadsheets and paper |
| **Counter-incentive strength** | **20%** | **the holder sells renewals and emails you** | mixed | **nobody holds the date, or the holder profits from you forgetting** |
| v1 buildability under our constraints | 10% | needs backend/accounts/licensed data | 2–3 weeks | ~1 week, rules free and stable |
| Reachability | 10% | diffuse, ads only | some trade groups | dense named channels we can post in |
| **Founder's contact proximity** | **10%** | **I know nobody** | one hop to the buyer | **my contacts ARE the buyer** |
| Willingness to pay a subscription | 5% | expects free | pays for tools | already pays a person for this job |

Weights sum to 100%. Note the two additions over the previous venture's rubric: **counter-incentive
strength** is weighted as heavily as pain, because it killed three of four finalists, and **contact
proximity** is scored because the founder's insider access is this venture's one structural
advantage and must be spent deliberately.

## Hard gates — any failure kills the niche at any score

1. Not an excluded market (`CLAUDE.md` §2).
2. **Counter-incentive gate:** the party holding the date must have no reason, or no ability, to
   notify the user. If they already email it, the niche is dead regardless of pain.
3. **Mechanic gate:** about twenty searches on the computation itself, in the customer's words,
   finding nothing that already performs it — free or paid, app or spreadsheet. Run this in
   Phase 1, before the deep dive.
4. Rule set public, free and stable **where the product computes from regulation**. No BS/BS EN or
   other paywalled standard as shipped data. Where the product instead organises what users enter,
   this gate does not apply — but record the absence of a rule set as a weakness, because it
   removes the cited-sources deliverable and the 60-second in-browser aha.
5. ~~Single-user and phone-first. No multi-user chasing workflow.~~ **REPLACED 2026-10-24 by
   founder decision — see `CLAUDE.md` §3.** Multi-party is now permitted. In its place, three
   gates: **(5a) self-serve** — payable by card by a firm under roughly 50 staff with no demo,
   pilot, procurement or security questionnaire; **(5b) single-player value** — the payer gets real
   value from their own use before any counterparty signs in, so no cold-start dependency;
   **(5c) one sharp job** — not a suite, and not useless without a second module.
6. App-Store-safe framing: organises the user's own data, computes from public rules, never
   certifies, advises or issues a verdict about a person, a machine or a site.
7. Recurring use over years, not once in a lifetime.
8. Not already nailed by a well-funded competitor.

10. **Third-party personal data.** A candidate whose backend would hold records about other
    people — a subcontractor's workers, their qualifications, their employment — carries real UK
    GDPR controller or processor duties. Score the cost in, and prefer designs that keep such data
    out of our systems. Not an automatic kill, but never a free pass. See `CLAUDE.md` §6.

**Tie-breakers, in order:** the aha moment can be delivered free in a browser in under 60 seconds;
the founder's own contacts can supply ten interviews; the rules change slowly; a concierge version
can be sold before the app exists.

## Founder inputs — frozen 2026-10-24, before any research returned

Recorded here, rather than in the weights, because the weights above are frozen. These resolve
ambiguity and set realistic ceilings; they do not re-weight anything.

| Input | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Roles reachable for warm conversations | Site managers / site agents **only**. Not subcontractor owners, not plant or yard managers, not commercial managers or QSs. |
| 8-week validation budget | £150–£400, organic channels, no paid ads |
| Risk appetite | Favour the odds of it working over eventual market size |

### What this does to the scoring

**1. Contact proximity: one hop is confirmed, so the cap lifts to 3 — and no higher.**
*Updated 2026-10-24, same day: the founder confirmed he knows a site manager who can introduce him
to a subcontractor owner.* The rubric's own scale puts "one hop to the buyer" at exactly **3**, and
reserves 5 for "my contacts ARE the buyer", which remains untrue. So a niche whose buyer is a
subcontractor owner now scores **3** rather than the 2 it was capped at an hour ago. That reopens
A1, A2 and B5, which are the candidates where the person who loses the money is the person who
would pay.

**The hop is a claim, not yet evidence.** A promised introduction and a completed one are different
things, and the previous venture died of assuming a channel worked. So this is now a cheap, dated
experiment rather than a scoring input we lean on: **one real introduction, completed, by
2026-10-31.** If it happens, contact proximity stays at 3 and the owner-operator candidates stay
live. If it does not, it drops back to 2 and the honest recommendation may be to stop. Do not
raise any score above 3 on the strength of a promise.

**2. Audience wealth now actively penalises the reachable audience.** The rubric scores "employee on
PAYE" as 1. That is precisely who we can reach. This is the central tension of this venture and it
must not be smoothed over: the group we have access to is the group least likely to pay personally.
A niche that resolves this — where the site manager genuinely buys for themselves, or where they
introduce us to someone who does — is worth more than a niche with bigger pain and no buyer.

**3. "Does the employer pay?" is promoted to a hard gate.** It was previously a lesson. Given the
answer above, it is now gate 9, because the failure mode is no longer hypothetical.

**4. Risk appetite resolves tie-breaks toward certainty.** Where two niches are within a few points,
prefer the one with the freer rule set, the faster aha moment and the clearer single-user story,
even if the market is visibly smaller. A small, real business is the stated goal. Do not inflate
pain £ to justify a bigger-looking market.

**5. The budget constrains Phase 2, not Phase 1.** £150–£400 rules out trade-show stands and paid
memberships as the primary channel. The validation plan must reach its thresholds on warm
introductions, free community posting and an in-browser aha moment.

### Hard gate 9 — the employer-pays gate

The person who loses money from the missed date must be the same person who would enter a card.
If the consequence lands on the company and the user is PAYE, the niche is dead regardless of pain,
unless the niche is credibly bought by an owner-operator we can reach in one hop. As of
2026-10-24 that one hop is confirmed as available, pending the completed-introduction experiment
above, so a subcontractor-owner buyer no longer fails this gate automatically.
